The clocks

Two kinds of clock run under this work. One is political and can be argued with: parliaments, terms, a Games in 2032. The other is the sky, and it cannot. Both are counting.

The political clock

Until the Brisbane opening ceremony

23 July 2032

The Brisbane 2032 Olympic Games run 23 July to 8 August 2032, with the Paralympic Games after them. A federal parliament runs at most three years, so at least two federal elections sit inside this window, and every state and territory goes to the polls as well. Live countdowns to each of those elections are on the P4A state and territory portal, and the regional cycles across the Pacific are on the Oceania pulse board.

What that clock is for is set out in the cyber republic room: a purple party winning seats, a simulator, a referendum carried outright, and Oceania able to join what comes out of it.

The bigger clock

Between 2023 and 2038 the Moon's shadow crosses the Australian region five times. Over the same period North America gets one, South America none, Europe two, Africa three and Asia two. Whatever else happens on this continent this decade, the sky is running an unusual sequence over it.

The first of the five has been and gone: Exmouth, 20 April 2023. Three are still ahead, and one of them lands five years after the Brisbane Games, over Brisbane.

22 July 2028

Until greatest eclipse

22 July 2028

Totality up to 5 minutes 10 seconds. Landfall in the Kimberley near Wyndham and Kununurra, across the Northern Territory by Tennant Creek, through south-west Queensland at Birdsville, then Bourke and Dubbo in New South Wales, over the centre of Sydney, and on to Queenstown and Dunedin in New Zealand.

Sydney sits in the path for a little under four minutes, in the early afternoon. The last total solar eclipse over that harbour was 171 years earlier. To watch the shadow move and get the times for your own street, open the interactive map.

25 November 2030

Until greatest eclipse

25 November 2030

Totality up to 3 minutes 44 seconds, in a band about 135 kilometres wide in South Australia narrowing to about 120 by south-east Queensland. The path crosses the South Australian coast near Streaky Bay, passes north of Adelaide, runs through the far north-west of New South Wales, and finishes at sunset about 150 kilometres north of Toowoomba, taking in Cunnamulla, Bollon, Surat and Miles on the way.

Adelaide is not in this one. The path passes to its north, which leaves the city in partial shadow and nothing more. The difference between the two is not a matter of degree. Check your own town on the interactive map.

13 July 2037

Until greatest eclipse

13 July 2037

Totality up to 3 minutes 58 seconds. Landfall near Geraldton in Western Australia, then across the southern Northern Territory past Yulara, into Queensland through Bedourie, Charleville, Roma and Toowoomba, directly over Brisbane and the Gold Coast, out through Byron Bay, then Lord Howe Island and the North Island of New Zealand.

Surat and Miles sit in the path of this one and the 2030 one both. Two totalities, seven years apart, over the same paddocks. The interactive map zooms to street level.

Where these come from

Eclipse dates, times of greatest eclipse and paths are from the Astronomical Association of Queensland, cross-checked against published eclipse catalogues, and the interactive maps linked above are timeanddate.com, which will also give you local circumstances for your own address. Times of greatest eclipse are given in universal time and the clocks above convert to wherever you are reading. Games dates are the announced Brisbane 2032 schedule.

A countdown is only as good as the moment it points at. These four are fixed and published: three by orbital mechanics, one by an organising committee. The first three will not move.
