Because it does.

In the summer of 2012 to 2013, one Queenslander pulled every piece of fine print and every act that touched his own life into one folder and read the lot, cover to cover, following the threads. Filed 2012 Read 2013 Overcomply

The story, straight

It started with a small tax form. Seven weeks later the folder held the Constitution, Magna Carta and fifty-odd acts besides: banking, tax, super, work, companies, welfare, police powers, tenancy, transport, elections, ships, treaties. The save-dates tell the story on their own. All five volumes of the Corporations Act went in on Christmas Day 2012 at 5.38pm. The five volumes of the Social Security Act followed two days later, in one sitting. A few more files joined in 2014, once the inventions needed patents and the health ideas needed the Therapeutic Goods Act.

Reading it produced two reactions in order. First: the urge to opt out of the whole machine. The folder still holds that road: Magna Carta, the Oaths Act, the American UCC-1 form the opt-out forums swore by. Reading the actual texts closed the road. The system is too well enforced to leave. There is no out. There is only through.

Through means overcomply. Know the rules better than the people administering them, meet every one of them, and use the same rules to change the rules. That decision, made in 2013, is the seed of everything on this site.

The cabinet, measured

61 documents in the 2012 folder: acts, regulations, treaties, forms and fine print. Enough paper, printed, to fill a decent wheelie bin.
~90 MB of law on disk. About one minute of phone video, holding the rules of a whole country's daily life. The heaviest single file: the Taxation Administration Act 1953, volume two.
9 parliaments and assemblies write Australia's statute law: the Commonwealth, six states and two territories. Each with its own register, its own drafting style, its own website.

The whole idea in four moves

  1. Gather

    Get the laws relevant to you. Not all of them. Yours. Start from your own wallet, lease, payslip and letterbox, and work outward.

  2. Chomp

    Read through them fast, with AI doing the heavy lifting and you doing the judging. What took a summer in 2013 can now take days.

  3. Sort

    Mark what serves you, what bites you, what is broken for people like you, and what is missing entirely. Your sort, not anyone else's.

  4. Plan

    Turn the sort into a plan to change what needs changing, using the system's own tools: forms, submissions, members, ballots.

"We don't need a riot, we don't need a fire. We need a robot lawyer that never gets tired." Don't Throw A Brick (Fill A Form), i C. infinity, 2025

The robot lawyer is the engine being designed here: one that reads all nine statute books, never fatigues, and is built so every answer can be checked against the exact section it came from. Never legal advice. Always a map to the source.
